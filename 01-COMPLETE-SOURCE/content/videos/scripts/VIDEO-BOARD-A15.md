Scenario: A board dashboard reports only course completion and total quiz points. Leadership asks whether the programme has reduced risky behaviour and improved incident readiness. Safest response: Option A: A balanced measurement set: pre/post knowledge, delayed retention, novel-scenario decisions, reporting behaviour, qualitative feedback and incident context, with limitations. A defensible programme uses multiple measures and avoids claiming that quiz scores alone prove real-world competency. Option B is unsafe because Completion is an activity measure and cannot by itself establish behaviour change or risk reduction. Option C is unsafe because Passing a quiz does not demonstrate prevention of real attacks or causal impact on incident rates. Option D is unsafe because A leaderboard may reflect participation or test-taking and does not show organizational risk outcomes. In practice: Report baseline and follow-up measures, delayed retention, scenario transfer, reporting quality and limitations. Use privacy-preserving aggregation and avoid unsupported causal claims. Key takeaway: Measure knowledge, retention and observed decisions with context; do not equate completion or quiz scores with security outcomes.
