## Learning objective
Interrupt payment diversion using independent verification, separation of duties and timely financial institution notification.

## Scenario
Finance receives an email in an existing supplier thread requesting that a large invoice be paid to a new bank account. The message appears to come from the supplier’s known address. The CFO separately messages the finance lead asking for the payment to be expedited and says the supplier is waiting.

## Narration transcript
Scenario: A supplier email thread requests a new bank account and an executive asks finance to hurry. Safest response: pause payment, independently verify with a previously known supplier contact and follow dual approval. Option A is unsafe because A compromised mailbox can control replies in the same email thread. Option C is unsafe because A test transfer can still be lost without authenticating the new account. Option D is unsafe because A familiar thread and executive urgency do not authenticate changed bank details. In practice: use trusted contact details, retain evidence and escalate; contact the bank promptly if funds moved. Key takeaway: never let urgency bypass payment controls.

## Correct response
B. Pause the payment and verify the change through a previously established, independently sourced supplier contact; require the organization’s documented dual-approval process and report the suspicious request through security and finance channels.

## Option-specific teaching notes
- **A:** A compromised mailbox can control replies in the same email thread. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **B (correct):** Correct. A trusted out-of-band verification path and separation of duties reduce the risk of payment diversion and create an auditable approval trail.
- **C:** A test transfer can still be lost without authenticating the new account. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **D:** A familiar thread and executive urgency do not authenticate changed bank details. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.

## Key takeaway
A payment-detail change is a high-risk request even when it arrives inside a familiar email conversation.

## Recommended defensive action
Place the payment on hold, call a previously verified supplier contact using a trusted directory or existing contract record, follow dual authorization, retain evidence and notify the organization’s security/finance process. If money has already moved, contact the bank immediately and report through the appropriate channels.
