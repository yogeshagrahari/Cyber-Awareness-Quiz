## Learning objective
Prioritize coordinated containment, evidence preservation and decision ownership during an active multi-system incident.

## Scenario
At 09:10, the security operations centre receives three correlated alerts: an endpoint has begun encrypting files, a service account has authenticated from an unusual location, and outbound traffic from a file server has increased sharply. The incident commander has not yet confirmed the initial access path. A business unit asks to power off every server immediately.

## Narration transcript
Scenario: Alerts show file encryption, unusual service-account use and unusual outbound traffic. The initial access path is unknown. Safest response: activate the incident plan, isolate confirmed affected assets, preserve evidence and scope related hosts and accounts. Option B is unsafe because A blanket shutdown can destroy volatile evidence and disrupt unaffected services. Option C is unsafe because Waiting for attribution can allow active damage to continue. Option D is unsafe because Direct uncoordinated contact bypasses the authorized response structure. In practice: appoint an incident lead, preserve logs, contain proportionately and record decisions and unknowns. Key takeaway: respond to credible indicators without waiting for perfect attribution.

## Correct response
A. Declare a major incident, activate the response plan, isolate confirmed affected systems using approved procedures, preserve relevant telemetry, and scope related accounts and hosts before broader disruptive action.

## Option-specific teaching notes
- **A (correct):** Correct. Coordinated activation, proportionate containment, evidence preservation and scoping support an informed response while limiting unnecessary operational disruption.
- **B:** A blanket shutdown can destroy volatile evidence and disrupt unaffected services. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **C:** Waiting for attribution can allow active damage to continue. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **D:** Direct uncoordinated contact bypasses the authorized response structure. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.

## Key takeaway
Act on credible indicators through the incident plan; do not wait for perfect attribution or take uncontrolled disruptive action.

## Recommended defensive action
Activate the approved incident-response process, appoint a decision owner, preserve logs and volatile evidence where feasible, isolate confirmed affected assets, scope identity and network activity, and record decisions and uncertainties.
