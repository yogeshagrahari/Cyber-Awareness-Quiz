## Narration transcript
Scenario: A product team requests a six-month MFA exception for a production administrator account to simplify deployment. No compensating controls or accountable risk owner are proposed. Safest response: Option C: Require documented business justification, scoped duration, risk-owner approval, compensating controls, monitoring and an expiry/review date before any exception. A controlled exception process preserves accountability and makes residual risk explicit. Option A is unsafe because Schedule pressure alone does not justify accepting an undocumented security risk. Option B is unsafe because Shared privileged accounts weaken accountability and do not solve the underlying control gap. Option D is unsafe because Some exceptions may be justified, but they require formal risk assessment, approval and compensating controls. In practice: Use the organization’s exception workflow, define the account and period in scope, assess residual risk, assign an accountable approver and set a review/expiry date. Key takeaway: An exception is a governed risk decision, not an informal bypass.
