# Research Search Log

**Search date:** 12 November 2026  
**Search type:** Initial rapid scoping search  
**Purpose:** Establish an evidence base before coding.

## Sources and discovery channels

The search used publisher and scholarly records discoverable through IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, ACM/DOI records, institutional repositories and broad scholarly discovery, together with primary standards and official guidance from NIST, OWASP, W3C, Google Be Internet Awesome and the Government of India's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.

Access varied by publisher. Some full-text pages returned access restrictions, so the synthesis relies only on information visible in accessible abstracts, records and institutional/publisher metadata. No unreported method or result is inferred.

## Inclusion principles

Included items were selected when they were peer-reviewed research, a systematic/scoping review, a standard or official guidance, or a primary official product source; directly relevant to awareness, phishing, serious games/AR/VR, AI-generated video, adaptive cybersecurity education, secure implementation or accessibility; and sufficiently documented to identify authorship, year and DOI/stable URL.

Vendor pages are used only to describe publicly marketed features, not as independent efficacy evidence.

## Exclusion and caution principles

- Marketing claims are not treated as peer-reviewed efficacy evidence.
- General-purpose VR studies are adjacent evidence, not direct evidence that browser-based 3D cybersecurity scenes improve learning.
- General AI-in-education findings are not automatically generalized to cybersecurity.
- Prototype satisfaction surveys are not treated as evidence of long-term behavioural change.
- A platform's existence does not prove effectiveness.
- No score improvement or behavioural outcome is attributed to CYBERAWARE 3D.

## Search limitations

This is not a PRISMA systematic review. The initial search did not create a complete database export or verify inter-reviewer agreement. Relevant studies may have been missed because of indexing, changing terminology, inaccessible full texts or search-string limitations. The 2026 game-based scoping review itself reports keyword/search-string, subjective quality-score and non-calculated inter-rater agreement limitations [9]. A formal review should preserve database exports, screening decisions, exclusion reasons and quality appraisal.

## Search conclusion

The initial corpus supports a design rationale for iterative, cognitively active, scenario-based learning and careful evaluation. It does **not** establish that adding 3D, one AI-generated video per question or a particular adaptive rule will independently improve real-world security behaviour.


## 7. Reproducibility, scope and known limits

This is a rapid scoping search snapshot, not a registered systematic review. Search engines and publisher indexing can change; database export files, duplicate-screening logs and two-reviewer screening were not produced in this first phase. The records selected for the current synthesis were verified against publisher, DOI, standards-body or official product pages where available. The search should be repeated before a submission, with a fixed date range, database-specific queries, inclusion/exclusion criteria, duplicate removal and a PRISMA-style flow record if the target venue expects systematic-review reporting.

Official standards and government guidance are used for technical recommendations; peer-reviewed studies are used for empirical claims; commercial vendor material is used only to describe vendor-stated functionality.
