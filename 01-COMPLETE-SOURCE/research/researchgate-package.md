## Abstract

Cybersecurity awareness programmes must help learners recognize evolving threats and make safe decisions, yet programme completion and short-term quiz performance do not necessarily demonstrate behaviour change. Prior research describes theory-based security training, anti-phishing education, serious games, augmented and immersive learning environments, and generative-AI-supported educational media. However, the evidence is heterogeneous: a recent scoping review of game-based cybersecurity awareness education identifies varied evaluation practices, while a large 2025 randomized field study reports limited practical value from commonly deployed annual and embedded phishing-training formats. Studies of AI-generated educational videos and generative AI in cybersecurity education identify opportunities alongside concerns about accuracy, hallucination, learner experience and limited evidence of sustained transfer.

This paper proposes CyberAware 3D, a research-traceable, progressive cybersecurity-awareness learning framework for non-specialist adult and undergraduate learners. It combines scenario-based questions, option-specific explanations, question-level AI-generated explanatory videos, selective browser-based 3D concept visualization, transparent adaptive recommendations and an auditable citation-to-content traceability model. A 2D alternative, captions, transcripts, reduced-motion support and privacy-conscious fictional simulations are treated as core requirements. A Design Science Research methodology is proposed, with controlled comparisons and measures for immediate knowledge, delayed retention, novel-scenario transfer, usability, accessibility and performance. No empirical results are claimed. The contribution is an integrated design and evaluation protocol intended to test whether these modalities provide measurable value beyond well-designed scenario instruction.

## Research objectives

1. Develop a source-backed cybersecurity threat taxonomy and learning-objective structure.
2. Create progressive Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced pathways with a defensive Expert Challenge.
3. Provide scenario decisions with correct- and incorrect-option explanations.
4. Bind questions, explanations and videos to research references using stable identifiers.
5. Develop a question-level video workflow with transparent asset-generation and review status.
6. Test selective 3D visualization against an accessible 2D alternative.
7. Implement explainable learning recommendations and spaced revision.
8. Evaluate immediate knowledge, retention, scenario transfer, usability and accessibility independently.

## Research contribution

The work proposes an integrated and traceable framework rather than claiming individual novelty for gamification, AI video or 3D visualization. Its main contribution is the end-to-end link from research evidence to learning objective, question, answer rationale, video, learner feedback and evaluation method.

## Research summary

The initial rapid scoping synthesis covers cybersecurity training, phishing education, game-based learning, AR/VR, AI-generated teaching videos, adaptive learning and applicable standards. It identifies evidence for active, theory-based instruction and contextual phishing education, while also highlighting that conventional annual/embedded training may have limited practical value. Existing serious games and immersive platforms demonstrate useful design patterns, but evaluation methods vary. AI video appears promising as a supplemental production method, but factuality and sustained learning remain concerns.

The current project package includes an IEEE-structured research/design manuscript, literature review, research-gap analysis, methodology, 38 numbered references, a 45 full question-item drafts (15 per core level), 45 explanation records, 45 question-specific narration scripts/storyboards/prompts/WebVTT caption drafts, a citation-to-feature traceability matrix and a locally runnable client-side demonstrator. Each draft remains pending expert content approval; no MP4 videos are generated. The demonstrator has automated unit/data/server tests, but browser visual E2E, manual WCAG review and learner evaluation remain incomplete. No rendered MP4 videos, experimental findings, publication or DOI are claimed.
