Scenario: A staff member with legitimate access downloads an unusually large volume of project files outside their normal work pattern. There is no evidence yet of data exfiltration or malicious intent. Safest response: Option A: Preserve relevant authorized telemetry and route the observable anomaly to the designated security/insider-risk process for proportionate review. Focus on verifiable behavior, policy and authorized investigation, not unsupported personal assumptions. Option B is unsafe because Unapproved surveillance or access can violate law, policy and privacy; follow authorized procedures. Option C is unsafe because Anomalous activity is not proof of intent; public accusation can harm fairness and compromise an investigation. Option D is unsafe because Authorized access can still be misused or become anomalous; the activity may warrant review. In practice: Preserve relevant logs according to policy, report through designated channels, limit information to need-to-know reviewers and involve HR/legal/privacy as required. Key takeaway: An indicator is a reason for proportionate review—not a diagnosis of motive or guilt.
