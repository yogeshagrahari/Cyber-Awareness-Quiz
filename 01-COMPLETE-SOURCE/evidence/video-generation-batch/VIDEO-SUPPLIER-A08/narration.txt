Scenario: A supplier asks for broad administrator access to your cloud tenant to troubleshoot an integration. The contract does not clearly describe data processing, logging or access termination. Safest response: Option A: Complete supplier/security review, define data and access scope, use named time-bound least-privilege accounts, log activity and document offboarding. Supplier access should be constrained, attributable, monitored and governed by explicit contractual and technical controls. Option B is unsafe because Broad access before due diligence expands exposure and can be difficult to unwind. Option C is unsafe because Shared credentials reduce accountability and create an insecure secret-distribution path. Option D is unsafe because A verbal promise without enforceable scope and technical controls is insufficient. In practice: Delay access until procurement, security, privacy and system owners document the supplier’s need, safeguards, data terms, account scope and revocation plan. Key takeaway: Third-party trust must be supported by evidence, scoped access, monitoring and lifecycle controls.
