Scenario: A risk register lists a low-likelihood event that could expose a large customer database. Existing controls are documented, but their current effectiveness has not been tested. Safest response: Option B: Assess likelihood and impact, validate existing control effectiveness, estimate residual risk and record assumptions and uncertainty. Risk assessment should consider both likelihood and consequence, then evaluate how controls change residual exposure. Option A is unsafe because Low likelihood alone ignores potentially severe impact and uncertainty about control effectiveness. Option C is unsafe because Automatic maximum ratings do not provide a consistent or useful risk prioritization method. Option D is unsafe because Documented controls are not necessarily effective; the risk remains until assessed and treated appropriately. In practice: Validate control operation, document evidence and uncertainty, calculate or qualitatively rate residual risk, then assign treatment and ownership. Key takeaway: Risk decisions require likelihood, impact, control evidence, residual risk and explicit assumptions.
