## Learning objective
Communicate incident facts, uncertainty, business impact and decisions to leadership without overstating attribution or assurance.

## Scenario
A board meeting is scheduled two hours after a suspected compromise of a customer-facing platform. The response team has isolated two hosts, but the scope of data access is still under investigation. The board chair asks for a definitive statement that no customer data was accessed and wants an exact recovery time.

## Narration transcript
Scenario: Two hosts are isolated after a suspected platform compromise, but data-access scope and recovery time remain unknown. Safest response: brief the board on verified facts, impacts, containment, uncertainties, owners and the next update. Option A is unsafe because No confirmed exfiltration does not prove that data was not accessed. Option B is unsafe because Leadership needs timely decision-ready information before attribution is complete. Option D is unsafe because Raw logs and attacker speculation can expose sensitive information and mislead leaders. In practice: separate facts, assumptions and unknowns, then record decisions and the update cadence. Key takeaway: communicate uncertainty clearly; do not promise what evidence cannot support.

## Correct response
C. Provide verified facts, known business impacts, containment already taken, current unknowns and confidence levels; explain next decision points, accountable owners, communication/legal review paths and a time for the next update rather than making unsupported assurances.

## Option-specific teaching notes
- **A:** No confirmed exfiltration does not prove that data was not accessed. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **B:** Leadership needs timely decision-ready information before attribution is complete. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.
- **C (correct):** Correct. Clear separation of verified facts, assumptions and unknowns supports governance decisions without overstating certainty.
- **D:** Raw logs and attacker speculation can expose sensitive information and mislead leaders. Review the evidence, use approved organizational controls and avoid treating an alert or familiar message as proof.

## Key takeaway
Good incident governance makes uncertainty visible and assigns decisions; it does not conceal uncertainty or turn it into assurance.

## Recommended defensive action
Use a structured incident briefing: what happened, what is confirmed, what remains unknown, business impact, actions taken, decisions needed, owner and next update time. Coordinate external and regulatory communications with authorized stakeholders.
