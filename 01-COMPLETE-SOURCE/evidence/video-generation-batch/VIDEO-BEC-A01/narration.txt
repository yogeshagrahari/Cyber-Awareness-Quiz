Scenario: A senior executive emails finance from a plausible account and asks for an urgent confidential transfer to a new beneficiary. The message says normal approval can be bypassed because the deal is sensitive. Safest response: Option C: Hold the transfer and verify through a pre-established independent channel and mandatory dual-authorization process. High-impact payment instructions require independent corroboration and policy-based approval, regardless of apparent seniority. Option A is unsafe because Authority and secrecy are not substitutes for identity verification or delegated financial authorization. Option B is unsafe because A smaller unverified transfer is still unauthorized and may confirm that the account is active. Option D is unsafe because A compromised mailbox can be controlled by the attacker, including replies in the same thread. In practice: Pause payment, use contact details already held in trusted records, validate the beneficiary independently and retain the approval trail. Key takeaway: For high-impact financial changes, verify identity and authorization outside the channel carrying the request.
