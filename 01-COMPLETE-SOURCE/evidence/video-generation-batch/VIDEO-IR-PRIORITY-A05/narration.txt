Scenario: An organization has two alerts: (A) malware is actively encrypting files on a shared operational server; (B) a single employee received a suspicious email but did not open it. Both require triage. Safest response: Option D: Prioritize the active encryption event for immediate coordinated containment while ensuring the email is reported and triaged. Active disruption and potential spread represent immediate operational risk; the lower-impact email still needs handling. Option A is unsafe because Delaying containment during active encryption may increase damage. Option B is unsafe because General prevalence does not outweigh evidence of active encryption and immediate operational impact. Option C is unsafe because Incidents differ in impact, scope, active harm and urgency; equal priority is not risk-based. In practice: Activate the incident-response plan, coordinate containment of the actively affected system, assign triage to the email and document priority rationale. Key takeaway: Prioritization considers current impact, scope, exposure, time sensitivity and containment opportunity.
