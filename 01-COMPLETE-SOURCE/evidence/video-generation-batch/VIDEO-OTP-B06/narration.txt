Scenario: A caller claiming to be bank support says a verification code has been sent to your phone and asks you to read it aloud to stop a suspicious transaction. Safest response: Option A: Hang up and contact the bank using the number on your card or official app; never share the code. An unsolicited caller must not receive an authenticator output; independently contact the institution. Option B is unsafe because Partial disclosure can still help an attacker complete an authentication flow. Option C is unsafe because Knowing personal details does not authenticate the caller; the code may authorize an action. Option D is unsafe because Changing the communication channel does not make disclosure safe. In practice: End the call and contact the bank through a known official channel. Review account activity and report the attempted social engineering. Key takeaway: An OTP is an authenticator output. Do not disclose it to callers, chat contacts or anyone else.
