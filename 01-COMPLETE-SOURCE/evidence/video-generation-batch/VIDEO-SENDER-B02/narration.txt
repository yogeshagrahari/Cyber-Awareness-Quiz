Scenario: An email displays the name “Campus IT Helpdesk,” but the full sender address ends in an unrelated public email domain. It asks you to confirm your password. Safest response: Option A: The full sender address and domain, checked against a known official contact. Inspect the full address and verify the domain using an independently known directory or channel. Option B is unsafe because Display names are easy to choose or imitate and do not authenticate the sender. Option C is unsafe because Timing is weak evidence; malicious messages can be sent at any time. Option D is unsafe because Logos can be copied and are not proof of message origin. In practice: Expand sender details, inspect the full address and domain, and contact IT through the published helpdesk channel if a credential request is unexpected. Key takeaway: A familiar name or logo is not identity proof; inspect and independently verify the actual address.
